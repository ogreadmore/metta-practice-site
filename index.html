The possibility

The mind can be trained so that love becomes its home.

Metta is not forced positivity or a demand to approve of everything. It is the deliberate training of goodwill: the sincere wish that beings be truly happy. The book follows that possibility through modern evidence, ancient history, the Metta Sutta, and a simple practice for daily life.

Evidence, with limits

Encouraging research. No guarantees.

Studies test structured exercises and programs, not promises. Effects vary, and results are less decisive against active alternatives than against waitlists.

2024 synthesis 23 randomized studies

Positive emotion, negative emotion, compassion, mindfulness, and psychological symptoms were among the measured outcomes.

Randomized field study Effects that can build

In working adults, gains in daily positive emotion predicted growth in personal resources such as purpose and social support.

Practical standard Test it directly

The evidence makes metta credible enough to practice seriously. It does not guarantee a particular result for every reader.

About the book

One practice. A lifetime of use.

Metta is the deliberate practice of goodwill. The book explains the science and history, reads the Metta Sutta closely, and teaches a method you can bring into meditation, work, relationships, conflict, ambition, and ordinary life.

Taylor Oliphant has studied meditation for more than fifteen years, including three years living in Nepal. He wrote this book to make the practice clear, credible, and genuinely useful.
